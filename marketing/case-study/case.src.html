galla is the Hindi word for a shop's cash box, the drawer every Indian shopkeeper guards. That is what this app is about: billing fast at the counter, and making sure the money in the drawer at night is the money the day earned.

Popular restaurant POS systems in India are priced and built for bigger outlets, with yearly contracts and their own hardware. A small dosa counter, a cafe or a family dine-in needs something simpler: correct GST bills, a UPI QR, kitchen slips, and a way to stop cash from quietly going missing.

The problem

Small restaurants pay big-restaurant prices for software they half use.

01

Cost. Several thousand rupees a year for software, plus add-ons and dedicated hardware.

02

Leakage. Cash goes missing between billing and closing, hidden behind discounts and cancelled bills.

03

Internet. The connection drops in the middle of the lunch rush, and cloud-only billing stops.

04

Complexity. Staff find dense screens slow and confusing, so they fall back to paper.

05

GST rules. Invoices need running numbers, CGST and SGST, and delivery-app orders follow different tax rules.

06

Month end. The CA asks for GST numbers, and the owner hands over a bag of bills.

The design question: how might we give a small restaurant honest, GST-correct billing on a phone they already own, even when the internet doesn't work?

Where galla wins

Lowest cost to start, no new hardware beyond a cheap printer, offline by design, and anti-leakage controls built in from day one.

Where it loses today

No inventory, recipes, loyalty, online ordering, multi-phone sync or Play Store presence. These are deliberate cuts for version one.

Who it's for

Three people share one phone.

A small restaurant has an owner who isn't always there, sometimes a manager, and a cashier who bills all day. Their needs pull in different directions: speed for the cashier, control for the owner.

Design principles

Six rules every screen had to pass.

Offline first

Every bill is saved on the phone before anything else. The internet is never needed to bill.

The phone they own

No billing machine, no tablet, no computer. A cheap 57 mm Bluetooth printer is optional.

Built against leakage

Personal PINs, approvals for discounts and cancellations, nothing deleted, blind cash counts.

GST done right

Invoice numbers per financial year, CGST and SGST on every bill, delivery-app orders taxed correctly.

Simple by design

Four bottom tabs, big touch targets, plain words. A new cashier should learn it in five minutes.

Free to run

Every service on a free plan. Low cost for us means a low price for a small restaurant.

The experience

A day at the counter, screen by screen.

Scroll sideways through the core screens. The sample shop is fictional.

Billing in three taps

  1. Tap the dish. Tiles show ADD, then turn into a −/+ stepper. Long-press marks a dish out of stock.
  2. Pick payment. UPI opens a QR with the exact amount, so the customer never types it.
  3. Print or WhatsApp. A proper tax invoice with GSTIN and FSSAI, and a daily token number.

Closing the day without accusing anyone

At night the app works out what should be in the drawer: start cash, plus cash bills, minus money paid out to vendors. A cashier counts without seeing that number, a blind count, so the result is honest. galla then says matches, short or extra, and the report can be printed or sent to the owner.

Key decisions

The calls that shaped it.

Save locally first, always

One SQLite database file per restaurant, on the phone. Billing never waits for a network.

Trade-off: no live multi-phone sync in version one; backups are a file the owner shares.

A maximum of four tabs

Orders, Bills, Insights, Profile. Everything else lives in Profile, grouped by who uses it.

Trade-off: owner-only tools are one level deeper, which keeps the cashier's view clean.

Blind count for cashiers

Cashiers count the drawer without seeing the expected total. Owners and managers see it.

Why: if the target is visible, a short drawer gets "found" money. Blind counts make the number honest.

Nothing is ever deleted

Cancelled bills stay visible, struck through, with who cancelled and why. Removed expenses stay on record.

Why: deletion is how leakage hides. A record that can't disappear protects honest staff too.

Delivery-app orders carry no GST

Zomato and Swiggy orders are entered with their order ID, duplicates are blocked, and no GST is added.

Why: under Section 9(5) the delivery platform pays GST on these orders, not the restaurant.

UPI QR only on the table bill

The printed QR appears on the pre-bill a guest sees at the table, not on the final tax invoice.

Why: the QR is for paying; once paid, the invoice should just say how it was paid.

"Rs" on paper, "₹" on screen

Receipts print amounts as Rs, 32 characters a line, ASCII only.

Why: many cheap thermal printers can't print the ₹ sign or a middle dot. Designing for the real printer beat designing for the mockup.

One owner, many restaurants

An early build blocked a second sign-up on the same phone. I reversed it: one phone can hold several restaurants, each its own file.

Why: owners often run two outlets. The first version assumed one restaurant per person, which was wrong.

How it was built

A designer and an AI, shipping a real app.

I owned the product: the problem, the flows, every screen decision, testing on real phones and a real printer. Claude wrote all the code in a cloud workspace and pushed every change to GitHub. I pulled it on my laptop, tested it in seconds, and turned it into an installable app with Expo's cloud build.

The loop

  1. I describe the change, or report what broke on my phone.
  2. Claude writes the code, type-checks it, and pushes to GitHub.
  3. I run git pull in Windows CMD and see it in Expo Go on the phone within seconds.
  4. For hardware features, Expo's cloud builds an APK that I install from a QR code.

Trust, security and privacy

A restaurant's sales are theirs.

Billing data is sensitive business information. I treated privacy as a design feature, not a legal page.

Encrypted on the phone

Each restaurant's database is encrypted with SQLCipher. Old files are converted with a row-count check, and left untouched if anything fails.

PINs, hashed

Every person has a unique PIN, stored only as a salted hash. Android phone backups are off so data can't be restored without its key.

Consent that means something

Terms and a Privacy Notice written to India's DPDP Act, with a Hindi summary. Contact permission is optional and off by default.

Customer numbers not kept

A guest's WhatsApp number only opens WhatsApp; it is never saved.

Crash reports stay local

Problem reports are saved on the phone without bills or customer details, and sent only if the owner chooses.

Rules for our own dashboard

The planned owner dashboard shows service health only. Restaurant numbers only with opt-in, and combined across five or more.

I tested on an iPhone through Expo Go and on an Android phone with the installed app and a BluPrints 57 mm printer, using a written test list. These are the problems testing found and how each was fixed.

What went wrong
What changed
The Offline chip never appeared on iPhone in airplane mode.
Airplane mode can keep Wi-Fi on. The app now checks real network state on a timer.
Bottom sheets were cut off and couldn't be dragged; the discount sheet opened in the middle of the screen.
One shared bottom sheet for the whole app: slides up from the bottom, swipe down to close.
Receipts lost their indentation, and "₹" and "·" printed as junk.
Leading spaces kept; printed text limited to plain characters, with "Rs" for rupees.
In grids of three, the last tile stretched to full width.
Invisible spacer tiles keep every tile the same size.
A second restaurant on the same phone was blocked as "already signed up".
Several restaurants per phone, one database file each, switched from the start page.
Windows said "driver unavailable" for the printer.
Not a bug: the phone talks to the printer directly over Bluetooth. Printing confirmed working.

Reflection

What I learned.

Design for the drawer, not just the screen

The hardest problems weren't layout. They were trust: who can give a discount, who sees the expected cash, what can never be deleted. Permission design is UX.

The real device is the real spec

A ₹ sign that looks perfect on screen prints as junk on a thermal printer. Airplane mode behaves differently on iPhone. Testing on real hardware changed decisions I thought were final.

Offline changes everything downstream

Choosing "save on the phone first" shaped backups, multi-restaurant support, encryption and the future cloud plan. One early principle made dozens of later decisions easy.

AI moves the bottleneck to judgement

With Claude writing the code, building stopped being the slow part. Deciding what to build, what to cut, and spotting what felt wrong on a real phone became the job.
