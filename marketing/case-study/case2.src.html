Overview

A cash box that keeps itself honest.

galla is the Hindi word for a shop's cash box, the drawer every Indian shopkeeper guards. The app is about exactly that: billing fast at the counter, and making sure the money in the drawer at night is the money the day earned.

Popular restaurant POS systems are priced and built for bigger outlets. A small dosa counter, a cafe or a family dine-in needs something simpler: correct GST bills, a UPI QR, kitchen slips, and a way to stop cash from quietly going missing.

Design principles

Six rules every screen had to pass.

Offline first

Every bill is saved on the phone before anything else. The internet is never needed to bill.

The phone they own

No billing machine, tablet or computer. A cheap 57 mm Bluetooth printer is optional.

Built against leakage

Personal PINs, approvals for discounts and cancellations, nothing deleted, blind cash counts.

GST done right

Invoice numbers per financial year, CGST and SGST on every bill, delivery-app orders taxed correctly.

Simple by design

Four bottom tabs, big touch targets, plain words. A new cashier learns it in five minutes.

Free to run

Every service on a free plan. Low running cost means a low price for a small restaurant.

Core flow

Closing the day without accusing anyone.

At night galla works out what should be in the drawer. A cashier counts without seeing that number, a blind count, so the result is honest. The app then says matches, short or extra, and the report can be printed or sent to the owner.

Key decisions

The calls that shaped it.

Save locally first, always

One database file per restaurant, on the phone. Billing never waits for a network.

Trade-off: no live multi-phone sync in version one; backups are a file the owner shares.

A maximum of four tabs

Orders, Bills, Insights, Profile. Owner tools live in Profile.

Trade-off: owner-only tools sit one level deeper, keeping the cashier's view clean.

Blind count for cashiers

Cashiers count the drawer without seeing the expected total.

Why: when the target is visible, a short drawer gets "found" money.

Nothing is ever deleted

Cancelled bills stay visible, struck through, with who cancelled and why.

Why: deletion is how leakage hides. A permanent record protects honest staff too.

Delivery-app orders carry no GST

Zomato and Swiggy orders are entered with their order ID; duplicates are blocked.

Why: under Section 9(5) the platform pays GST on these orders, not the restaurant.

UPI QR only on the table bill

The printed QR appears on the bill a guest sees at the table, not on the final invoice.

Why: the QR is for paying; once paid, the invoice just says how.

"Rs" on paper, "₹" on screen

Receipts print 32 characters a line, plain characters only.

Why: many cheap thermal printers can't print ₹. Design for the real printer.

One owner, many restaurants

An early build blocked a second sign-up on one phone. I reversed it.

Why: owners often run two outlets. Each restaurant now gets its own file.

Trust and privacy

A restaurant's sales are theirs.

Billing data is sensitive business information, so privacy was designed in, not written up afterwards.

Encrypted on the phone

Each restaurant's data is encrypted. Old files are converted with a row-count check, and left untouched if anything fails.

PINs, hashed

Every person has a unique PIN, stored only as a salted hash. Phone backups are off, so data can't be restored without its key.

Consent that means something

Terms and a Privacy Notice written to India's DPDP Act, with a Hindi summary. Contact permission is optional.

Customer numbers not kept

A guest's WhatsApp number only opens WhatsApp. It is never saved.

Crash reports stay local

Saved on the phone without bills or customer details, and sent only if the owner chooses.

Rules for our own dashboard

The planned dashboard shows service health only; restaurant numbers only with opt-in, combined across five or more.

The owner picks a plan, pays a UPI ID with a reference and enters the UPI transaction number. Once checked and approved, the plan switches on. Data is never deleted when a plan lapses.

Reflection

What I learned.

Design for the drawer, not just the screen

The hardest problems were about trust: who can give a discount, who sees the expected cash, what can never be deleted. Permission design is UX.

The real device is the real spec

A ₹ sign that looks perfect on screen prints as junk on a thermal printer. Airplane mode behaves differently on iPhone. Real hardware changed decisions I thought were final.

One early principle decides a hundred later ones

"Save on the phone first" shaped backups, multi-restaurant support, encryption and the cloud plan. Getting it right early made later choices easy.

Real testing beats polishing in the dark

Testing a working app on real phones and a real printer surfaced problems no mockup review would have found.
